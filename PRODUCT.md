## Product Purpose

ADI Workflow is the scheduling and crew-management system for ADI Productions,
a live-event AV production company. It replaces spreadsheets and disconnected
documents with one place that holds each show's day-by-day timeline and
generates everything downstream from it: call sheets, departmental
sub-schedules (the OSS and its department tabs), crew rosters, travel and
hotel logistics, hours reports, and client-facing exports. **[repo]**

Success, in Jason's words, is three things: **[Jason]**

1. **Everything ships from the app.** Call sheets, OSS, crew lists and travel
   go out from ADI Workflow, with no side spreadsheets.
2. **Larry can run a show day without asking Jason.** Nothing on screen needs
   its author to decode it.
3. **Hours and payroll come straight off the schedule.** Crew hours, double
   time and turnaround must be trustworthy enough to pay people from.

## Positioning

One timeline, five reflections. The day schedule is the single source; the
OSS master, the department tabs, the PDF export and the XLSX export are views
of it. Jason's standing rule: "The OSS and its tabs are just a reflection of
the day schedule, so anything you change should encompass all 3 areas of the
site." Appearance changes go through `brand.row_code(kind, dept)` and
`templates/_row_kinds.html`, never one template. **[repo — project docs quote
Jason]**

## Operating Context

- **Where:** in venues, on laptops, during load-in and show days, often
  minutes before a crew call. **[Jason]**
- **Show rhythm:** work lands between shows. No template or design changes
  during a show week; the next show, MCDC26, loads in 2026-09-08 and runs to
  about 2026-09-17. Design work for this cycle is **plan-only** (init, audit,
  critique) until after that. **[Jason, 2026-09-05]**
- **Density is the job, not a preference.** A crew call routinely carries 40+
  rows that all need to be visible. **[repo — brief; consistent with day.html]**
- **Print and export are first-class.** Screens are printed constantly, in
  black and white, on US Letter. PDFs are generated by ReportLab (`oss_pdf.py`),
  spreadsheets by openpyxl (`oss_xlsx.py`); print specs must be expressible as
  table styles and cell fills, not CSS. **[repo]**
- **Deployment:** production on PythonAnywhere at
  killingthemains.pythonanywhere.com; local copy under launchd on port 8080
  with a separate database. No login — the public URL is used by a small
  trusted team. **[repo]**
- **Terminology Larry uses** (keep it): show, day page, crew call, crew row,
  section (crew grouped by company), local labor ("18 × Lighting Hand" —
  tracked by position, not name), break period, beverage service, recurring
  event, start/end-of-day anchors, OSS (on-site support: F&B, COMS,
  wristbands), department tabs, call sheet and call sheet packet, phase types
  (the `07 Lists` vocabulary), Vendor Job Number, RFQ. **[repo]**

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Capabilities [repo]:** shows and clients; day timelines with activities,
crew calls, local labor blocks, breaks, beverage services, recurring events
and anchors; drag-to-reorder crew rows and activities; copy a crew call to
other days; crew database with spreadsheet import (column mapping, preview,
open-slot placeholders); crew travel and hotel dates with a bulk editor; hours
report; OSS master and department tabs with section PDF export and XLSX
export; call sheets (single and multi-day packet); day templates (currently
flagged — they write breaks the old way); an in-app request/bug board; audit
trail with undo.

**Engineering constraints — how the app is built today. Keep unless Jason
says otherwise.** Jason approved recording these as codebase facts rather
than as his decisions. **[repo, recording approved by Jason]**

1. No build step. Flask + Jinja2 + Bootstrap 5 from a CDN + one hand-written
   stylesheet (`static/css/style.css`, plus `paper.css` for the standalone
   print surfaces) + SortableJS. Vanilla JavaScript only, as little as
   possible. No browser storage for app data — the only use is the
   scroll-restore in `base.html`, which keeps a scroll position in
   `sessionStorage` for five seconds across a POST. (The stale brief said
   "none anywhere"; that was wrong about the code.)
2. Structure that must not be touched: the crew row
   `<tr>` / `<form style="display:contents">` / `<td>` chain; `form="id"`
   associations that wire forms living outside their tables; the `⠿` drag
   handles and their target size; the timeline row order (a test walks every
   row and requires non-decreasing times). ~2,900 lines of `day.html` use
   inline `style="..."` with CSS custom properties — restyle by changing
   variable values, never by renaming variables.
3. Print is first-class (see Operating Context).
4. WCAG AA, with the contrast ratio stated for every colour pair specified.
   Row-kind meaning must survive greyscale and colour-blindness — hue alone
   cannot carry it.
5. `--adi-gold` (`#7A5C1E`) is an INK colour for text; `--adi-gold-lt`
   (`#C9A45C`) is the fill. Guard tests exist for this and for inline JS
   handlers (`js_str` filter). Emoji and pictographs are banned in templates;
   use `ico.icon(...)`.

## Product Principles

1. **Design for Larry alone, in the dark, in a hurry.** If it needs Jason to
   explain, it has failed.
2. **The timeline is the truth; everything else reflects it.** A change to
   the day page is a change to all five surfaces or it is not done.
3. **Dense, not cramped.** Forty rows visible beats forty rows comfortable;
   but every row must still be legible on a laptop in a dark venue and on a
   mono laser printer.
4. **Trustworthy enough to pay from.** Anything touching hours, breaks or
   turnaround is built only on Larry's confirmed rules, never on assumptions.
5. **Cheap to redo.** The brand is provisional; prefer decisions a designer
   can overrule without a rebuild.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA for all text, including the small text that dense tables require.
Row kinds must be distinguishable in greyscale and to colour-blind users —
shape, rail, weight or label must carry meaning alongside colour. Print
parity: what reads on screen must read on a black-and-white US Letter page.
